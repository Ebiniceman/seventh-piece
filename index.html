An open-source chess experiment

A new piece.
A different game.

The Seventh Piece is an experimental chess variant built around a special piece that begins inactive and enters play only when a key condition is met. It explores strategic reversals, sacrifice, and the choices that emerge when familiar rules change.

The idea

Rules that create new questions.

What if a piece could enter only after the board changes? What if a limited reversal could alter how players value risk? The project is a work in progress, designed to invite testing, debate, and improvement rather than claim a finished rule system.

♟

Conditional activation

The Seventh Piece starts inactive. In the current prototype, it appears when the king or queen leaves its original square.

↶

Strategic reversal

The proposed rules include limited move reversals for specific high-threat situations. The full special-condition logic is not yet implemented.

◇

Open collaboration

The project is intended to be explored in the open: test the mechanics, find edge cases, and suggest clearer, fairer rules.

Prototype rules

What is proposed—and what works today.

The design is still experimental. These notes distinguish the creator’s proposed mechanics from the simplified implementation in the current browser demo.

01 · Entry condition

Proposed: the piece is inactive until the king or queen vacates its original square. The current demo uses that condition to place the piece on the vacated square.

02 · Diagonal movement

Proposed and prototyped: the Seventh Piece can move one square diagonally in any direction, subject to the demo’s simplified move logic.

03 · Limited reversals

Proposed: up to three reversals for a move threatening two rooks, plus one reversal for a queen-threat move. These special limits and threat checks are not yet implemented.

04 · Further edge cases

Other proposed interactions include conditions involving knights, bishops, pawns, checks, and the loss of a queen. They need formal specification and testing before they can be treated as implemented rules.

Prototype limitation: The current demo is not a complete chess engine. It does not yet enforce check/checkmate, castling, en passant, or full king-safety legality. Its undo control is a generic last-move undo, not the special reversal system described above.

Play it. Question it. Help shape it.

A small, mobile-friendly browser prototype is included with this package. It runs locally and does not require an account or network connection. Feedback from chess players and developers is welcome.
